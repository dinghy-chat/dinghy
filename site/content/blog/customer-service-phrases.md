# Customer service phrases for live chat

The best customer service phrases in live chat are short, specific and sound like one person talking. The worst ones come from call-center scripts: "We apologize for any inconvenience", "Your satisfaction is our priority", "Is there anything else I can help you with today?" A visitor reads those as a sign that nobody's really there.

![Two speech bubbles between a harbor house and a sailboat, joined by a line of signal flags](/img/illustrations/customer-service-phrases.png)

Small teams have an advantage here. The visitor knows they might be talking to the founder. Write like it.

## The rules behind the phrases

Most good chat phrases follow four habits:

- Answer first, then explain. The visitor asked a question. The first line is the answer.
- Say what happens next, with a time. "I'll check" means nothing. "I'll check and reply here by 3 p.m." is a promise they can hold you to.
- Use their words. If they wrote "the export button", don't call it "the data portability feature".
- One message, one idea. A wall of text in a chat window gets skimmed. Two short messages get read.

The rest of this post is examples.

## Opening the chat

The visitor already wrote their question. Don't make them wait through a greeting.

**Before**
> Hello and thank you for contacting Acme Support! My name is Sam and I'll be assisting you today. How may I help you?

**After**
> Hi, Sam here. Yes, you can change the plan anytime. Settings > Billing, then "Change plan".

If you need a minute, say so:

> Hi, Sam here. Let me look at your account. Give me 2 minutes.

## Asking for details

Ask for everything you need in one message. Three rounds of "can you tell me X?" feels like an interrogation.

**Before**
> Can you please provide your email address?
>
> (later) And what's your order number?
>
> (later) Which browser are you using?

**After**
> Let's sort this out. Can you send me the email on the account, the order number, and which browser you're on?

If you already know something, don't ask. Most chat tools, Dinghy included, show you the pages the visitor saw, their browser and any email they already gave. Asking for what's on your screen tells them you didn't look.

## Saying sorry

Apologize once, for the specific thing, then fix it. "Sorry for the inconvenience" apologizes for nothing in particular.

**Before**
> We sincerely apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused.

**After**
> Sorry, that's our bug. The invoice shows the wrong tax rate. I've fixed it, and the corrected invoice is in your account now.

When it's not your fault, you can still be sorry about the situation without taking blame you'll regret:

> That's annoying, sorry. I checked, and your bank declined the payment, so nothing was charged. Can you try once more, or use another card?

## Saying no

Small teams say no a lot: no phone support, no custom plan, no refund after 60 days. Say it plainly, give the reason in one line, and offer what you can do.

**Before**
> Unfortunately, that is not something we are able to accommodate at this time due to our current policies.

**After**
> Our refund window is 60 days, and your renewal was 68 days ago, so I can't refund it. What I can do is pause your plan for 3 months at no cost.

Drop "unfortunately". It adds a word and delays the answer.

## When you don't know yet

Don't guess, and don't stall with "let me look into that for you" with no time attached.

**Before**
> Let me look into that for you.

**After**
> I don't know yet. I'm asking our developer and I'll reply here by tomorrow noon.

Then keep the promise. If tomorrow noon comes and you still don't know, write anyway: "Still on it, no answer yet. Next update Friday."

## Handing off to a teammate

Tell the visitor who's taking over and why. Don't make them repeat the story.

**Before**
> I will transfer you to the appropriate department.

**After**
> Billing is Lea's area, so I've passed this to her with everything you told me. She's online and will reply here in a few minutes.

## Angry visitors

Don't match their tone, and don't use calming phrases from a script. "I understand your frustration" reads as "I was trained to say this". Show that you understood by naming the problem.

**Before**
> I completely understand your frustration and I'm here to help.

**After**
> You were charged twice for November. That's wrong, and I've refunded the second charge. Your bank can take a few days to show it.

If you can't fix it on the spot, say what you're doing and when you'll be back.

## Closing the chat

Don't ask "Is there anything else I can help you with today?" They'll tell you. Close with what they need to know after the chat ends.

**Before**
> Is there anything else I can help you with today? Thank you for choosing Acme, have a wonderful day!

**After**
> Done, the domain is connected. It can take up to an hour to work everywhere. If it still doesn't load tonight, reply here.

## Phrases to drop

| Drop | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "We apologize for any inconvenience" | Apologizes for nothing specific | "Sorry, that's our bug" |
| "Your call is important to us" | It's a chat, and nobody believes it | Answer the question |
| "Unfortunately" | Delays the answer | Start with the answer |
| "Let me look into that for you" | No time, no commitment | "I'll check and reply by 3 p.m." |
| "I understand your frustration" | Sounds scripted | Name the problem |
| "As per our policy" | Hides behind a rule | Give the reason in one line |
| "Please be advised" | Nobody talks like this | Say the thing |
| "Is there anything else?" | Ends the chat on a form question | Say what happens next |
| "No problem!" after a complaint | It was a problem for them | "Done" or "Fixed" |

## Phrases that work

These are worth saving:

- "Good question." Only when it is one, and never twice in one chat.
- "Here's the link:" followed by a direct link to the exact page, not the help center home.
- "That's on us."
- "I've done it for you." Better than telling them how, when you can.
- "I'll reply here by [time]."
- "If you need to go, I'll post the answer here. Want me to email it too?" Offer email only if your chat tool can send it.
- "Want me to [specific next step]?"

## Save your best replies

Once you've typed the same answer twice, save it. Most chat tools have saved replies, also called canned responses. In Dinghy they live in **Settings > Saved replies**, and you insert one in a conversation by typing **/** and its shortcut, such as `/refund-policy`. If you connect your own AI agent, it can read them before it drafts, so its drafts use your team's words. [Chatbot vs live chat](/blog/chatbot-vs-live-chat) covers how that works.

A saved reply should still read like a person wrote it. Keep each one short, and edit it to fit the chat before you send. Dinghy's saved replies have no placeholders for the visitor's name, because most visitors never give one, so write them to work without it.
