# Dinghy vs Intercom

Intercom puts an AI agent at the center of its customer service software. Dinghy is a small live chat with a shared inbox. If you're one to five people answering chats yourselves, use Dinghy.

## The quick answer

Use Dinghy for website chat, a shared inbox and alerts, at one price for the team. Use Intercom for its AI agent, product tours, help center, email and phone support.

## Price for a small team

| | Dinghy Cloud | Intercom Essential |
|---|---|---|
| 1 person | $7 a month | $19 to $29 a month |
| 3 people | $7 a month | $57 to $87 a month |
| 5 people | $7 a month | $95 to $145 a month |
| AI answers | No AI fee. Your own agent connects and drafts replies | $0.99 per Fin outcome, if you turn it on |
| Self-hosting | Free, open source | Not possible |
| Free trial | None | 14 days |

Intercom prices are from intercom.com/pricing on November 8, 2026. The list price is $29 a seat, new customers see $19, and startups can apply for a bigger discount. [All the prices side by side](/compare/).

For three people, Dinghy costs $50 to $80 less a month, depending on your Intercom price.

## What Intercom does that Dinghy doesn't

- Fin, a hosted AI agent that learns from your help center and answers customers by itself.
- A help center, product tours, banners and tooltips.
- Email, phone and other channels in the same inbox.
- Workflows, routing rules, advanced reporting and a big app marketplace. Dinghy has analytics, an API, webhooks and an MCP server, but no marketplace.

## What Dinghy does differently

Intercom's bill grows with every seat, and with every Fin outcome if you use Fin. Dinghy Cloud is $7 a month for the workspace, up to 5 people. Your second support person costs nothing. You pay at signup and can cancel anytime.

Dinghy has no AI of its own. If you want a bot, connect your own agent through MCP or the API. Its replies wait as drafts until a person sends them, unless you let it send on its own. And you pay your model provider, not me. For the setup, see [Bring your own AI agent](/blog/bring-your-own-ai-agent). It's open source, so you can always leave: export your conversations, contacts and settings to a file and import them into a [self-hosted Dinghy](/open-source-live-chat), which is free.

## Switching from Intercom

There's no importer from Intercom. The Dinghy tag goes where the Intercom snippet was. The steps are in [Moving over from another chat](/compare/#moving-over-from-another-chat).

If you used Intercom's identity verification to show signed-in users, Dinghy works the same way. Your server signs a short token, and operators see who they're talking to. It's in [Show who is chatting](/docs/add-to-your-site#show-who-is-chatting).
