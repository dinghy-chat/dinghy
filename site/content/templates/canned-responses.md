# Canned responses that don't sound canned

A canned response is a reply you write once and reuse whenever the same question comes in. Below are 60 of them, grouped by situation, ready to copy. If you've typed the same answer three times this week, it should probably be one of these.

![A hand picks a message bubble from a crate of ready replies on a dock, next to a small sailboat](/img/illustrations/canned-responses.png)

The catch is that a reply people can tell is canned makes them feel processed. So the examples here are short and plain, and each group says what to change before you send.

## What canned responses are

You'll also hear them called saved replies, macros, snippets or quick replies. Same thing. You store a block of text, give it a short name, and insert it into a reply with a shortcut.

They're good for answers that don't change between customers. Your refund policy, how to reset a password, your shipping times, what to send when you need a screenshot. They're bad for anything where the customer is upset, or where the answer depends on their account. Use those as a starting point and rewrite half of it.

## How to write ones that don't sound canned

- Write the way you'd talk to the customer across a counter. Read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it, change it.
- Lead with the answer. "Yes, you can change your plan any time" beats two lines of thanks first.
- Keep it to two or three sentences in chat. People read chat on a phone, between other things.
- Cut the stock phrases. "We apologize for any inconvenience", "Your satisfaction is our top priority", "Please do not hesitate to reach out". Nobody has ever felt better reading them.
- Say sorry once, about the specific thing. "Sorry the export broke your report" works. "We sincerely apologize" doesn't.
- Leave a gap to fill. A reply with the customer's order number or the name of the page they were on reads as written for them, even if 90% of it is saved.
- Give one next step. Either you do something, or they do something. Say which, and when.
- Review the set every few months. Prices, policies and screens change, and an old saved reply that's wrong is worse than none.

## The placeholders

Words in curly brackets are the parts you fill in before you send:

- `{name}` is the customer's first name. If you don't have it, write "Hi there," instead of "Hi {name},". In a line like "I'm sorry, {name}.", drop the comma and the name.
- `{your name}` is your own first name.
- `{order number}`, `{plan}`, `{amount}`, `{date}` and `{link}` are what they say.
- `{time}` is a real time, like "3pm" or "9am Monday". Add the time zone if the customer might be somewhere else.
- `{days}` is the refund time your payment provider gives, like "5 to 10".
- `{issue}` is a few words that name the problem in the customer's own terms.
- `{teammate}` is the first name of the person you hand the chat to.
- `{reason}`, `{alternative}`, `{workaround}` and `{feature}` are the specifics of your answer. Never send these vague. "Because of our policy" isn't a reason.

If you paste these into a tool that fills fields on its own, swap the brackets for its own variable format. In Dinghy you fill them by hand, because saved replies have no placeholders. Most chat visitors never give a name, so a reply that fills one in would often say "Hi ,".

## Greetings

Use these as the first reply when a chat starts. If the visitor already asked a question, skip the greeting and answer it. A greeting followed by "how can I help?" when they just told you how is the most canned thing in support.

```template
Hi {name}, I'm {your name}. What can I help with?
```

```template
Hey! I'm here. What's going on?
```

```template
Hi, thanks for writing in. Give me a minute to read this properly and I'll get back to you.
```

```template
Hi {name}, good to see you again. Is this about the same thing as last time, or something new?
```

```template
Hi {name}, thanks for waiting. I'm reading through your message now.
```

```template
Hi! I saw you were on the pricing page. Happy to answer anything about plans, or I'll leave you to it.
```

## Asking for details

Use these when you can't answer yet. Ask for everything you need in one message, so the customer doesn't feel like they're filling out a form one field at a time.

```template
I can help with that. Which email address is your account under?
```

```template
Can you send me a screenshot of what you're seeing? It helps me find the problem faster. Crop out anything private first.
```

```template
Thanks, a couple of quick questions so I don't send you down the wrong path:
1. What browser and device are you on?
2. When did this start?
3. Does it happen every time, or only sometimes?
```

```template
What's your order number? It starts with # and it's in your confirmation email.
```

```template
Just to make sure I've got it right, you want to {what they want to do}. Is that it?
```

```template
Which page were you on when this happened? The page name is enough. Please don't send a sign-in or password reset link.
```

## Bugs and technical problems

Use these when something is broken on your side. Say plainly that it's a bug when it is one. People forgive bugs much faster than they forgive being told it's their fault.

```template
That's a bug on our side, not something you did. I've passed it to the team with your details, and I'll message you here when it's fixed.
```

```template
I can reproduce it, thanks for the clear steps. While we fix it, you can get around it by {workaround}.
```

```template
I can't make it happen on my end yet. Could you try it in a private window? If it works there, the problem is likely an extension or something your browser saved, and either way it tells me where to look.
```

```template
We know about this one and we're working on it now. You can follow it at {link}. I'll also write to you when it's done.
```

```template
Good news, the fix for {issue} is live. Could you refresh the page and try again? Tell me if it's still acting up.
```

```template
I've looked at your account and I don't see anything wrong yet. I'm handing it to someone who knows this part of the product better. They'll reply here by {time}.
```

## Billing and refunds

Use these for charges, plan changes and refunds. Always say the amount and the date. "We've processed your refund" without a number makes people check their bank every hour. For longer refund emails, see the [refund email templates](/templates/refund-email-template).

```template
Done, I've refunded {amount} to your card. It should show up in {days} business days, depending on your bank.
```

```template
You were charged {amount} on {date} for the {plan} plan. The invoice is at {link}.
```

```template
I see two charges on {date}. The second one is a mistake on our side, and I've refunded it now.
```

```template
You can change your plan any time in your account settings, under billing. The new price starts on your next bill.
```

```template
I've cancelled your subscription. You keep access until {date}, and you won't be charged again.
```

```template
I can't refund this one, because {reason}. What I can do is {alternative}. Would that help?
```

```template
Your card was declined, so the payment didn't go through. You can update your card at {link}. Your account stays open while you do.
```

## Angry customers

Use these as the first line, then write the rest yourself. Don't send an angry customer a fully saved reply. They can tell, and it makes things worse. Agree with what's true, own your part, and say what happens next. More on this in [customer service response templates](/templates/customer-service-response-templates).

```template
You're right, that shouldn't have happened. I'm sorry. Here's what I'm going to do about it.
```

```template
I get why you're frustrated. You've had to explain this more than once, and that's on us. I'm taking it from here.
```

```template
I'm sorry, {name}. Let me look at your account properly before I say anything else. Give me two minutes.
```

```template
That's a fair complaint, and I'm not going to argue with it. Can you tell me what would make this right for you?
```

```template
I think a call would sort this faster than more messages. Can I ring you? If you'd rather not, I'll write up here what I'll do and by when.
```

## Delays and waiting

Use these when you need time. The goal is that the customer never wonders if you forgot them. Give a real time frame, and if you miss it, write before it passes, not after.

```template
Give me a few minutes to look into this. I'll stay in this chat.
```

```template
This needs a bit more digging. I'll get back to you by {time}, even if it's just to say I'm still on it.
```

```template
Thanks for waiting. Still checking with the team, and I'll update you by {time}.
```

```template
Sorry for the slow reply. We had more chats than usual today. Here's the answer to your question:
```

```template
I'm checking with someone who knows this better, and they're out until {date}. I didn't want to leave you without an answer until then.
```

## Closing the conversation

Use these when the problem is solved. Check that it is solved before you close. A closing line that ends a chat the customer still needed is worse than no closing line.

```template
Glad that's working now. Anything else while I'm here?
```

```template
Sounds like we're sorted. If it happens again, reply here and I'll pick it up.
```

```template
I'll close this chat for now. You can write back here any time, and your history stays with it.
```

```template
Thanks for your patience with this one, {name}. Have a good rest of your day.
```

```template
I haven't heard back in a while, so I'll close this for now. If it's still not sorted, reply here and we'll pick it up again.
```

## After hours

Use these when someone writes while nobody is working. Say when you'll be back, and ask for an email so the reply can reach them. More in [holiday and away messages](/templates/holiday-messages).

```template
Hi, we're offline right now and back at {time}. Leave your question and your email, and we'll reply first thing.
```

```template
Thanks for writing. It's after hours here, but a real person will read this by {time}. Leave your email so we can reply, and your order number if it's about an order.
```

```template
We're closed for the weekend and back at {time}. Your message is saved, and if you leave your email, the reply goes there too.
```

```template
Sorry for the wait, we were closed when you wrote. Here's the answer:
```

```template
Hi {name}, you wrote to us last night about {issue}. I've looked into it, and here's where it stands.
```

## Handing off to a teammate

Use these when someone else is better placed to answer. Tell the customer who, why, and when they'll hear back. Then leave your teammate a note with what you already know, so the customer doesn't have to repeat themselves.

```template
This one's for {teammate}, who handles billing. I've passed on everything you told me, so you won't need to repeat it. They'll reply here by {time}.
```

```template
I'm handing you over to {teammate}, who built this part of the product. They'll take it from here.
```

```template
Hi {name}, {teammate} told me about {issue}. I've read the chat, so you don't need to explain it again.
```

```template
I'm off for the day, so {teammate} will pick this up. I've left them a note with where we got to.
```

## Live chat specific

Use these for things that only happen in chat. People switch tabs, go quiet, or open a chat by accident. Keep these extra short.

```template
Still there? No rush, I'll keep this open for a bit.
```

```template
Looks like you stepped away. I'll close this for now, and you can reply any time to pick it back up.
```

```template
Just checking, did that answer your question?
```

```template
I'm typing up the steps for you now, one moment.
```

```template
This is easier to show than explain. Here's a screenshot of where to click:
```

```template
Hi, I noticed you've been on the setup page for a while. Stuck on something? I can walk you through it.
```

```template
Thanks for the chat! If you'd rather get the answer by email, leave your address and I'll send it there too.
```

## Saying no

Use these when the answer is no. Say no in the first sentence, give the real reason, and offer what you can. Hiding the no under three lines of thanks makes people read it twice.

```template
We don't have that yet, sorry. I've added your vote to the request, and I'll tell you if it ships.
```

```template
That's not something we can do, because {reason}. A lot of customers use {alternative} for it instead.
```

```template
No, that plan doesn't include {feature}. It's on {plan}, which is {amount} a month.
```

```template
I'd like to say yes, but I can't make an exception on this one. What I can do is {alternative}.
```

## Saved replies in Dinghy

Dinghy has [saved replies](/docs/operator-guide#saved-replies) in Settings > Saved replies. Each one has a shortcut, such as `refund-policy`, and a text. In the reply box, type **/** and a few letters of the shortcut, then press Enter to put the text in the box. You can edit it there before you send. Everyone on the team can add, change and delete them, and a workspace keeps up to 200.

There are no placeholders, so leave the `{brackets}` in and fill them in by hand, or write the saved reply without them. Your own AI agent can read the same saved replies through the API and MCP, so a bot answers in your team's words too.

## Questions people ask

### What is a canned response?

A reply you write once and reuse for a question that keeps coming in. You insert it with a shortcut instead of typing it again. Saved replies, macros and snippets are other names for the same thing.

### How many canned responses should a small team have?

Start with 10 to 20, one for each question you answered more than twice last month. Look at your recent chats and copy your best answers. A set of 200 that nobody remembers is less useful than 15 that everyone uses.

### How do I make canned responses sound less robotic?

Make them shorter, cut the stock apologies, and leave one gap to fill with something about this customer. Then change one sentence before you send. That's usually enough.

### Should I use canned responses with angry customers?

Use one for the first line at most. An upset customer can tell a saved reply, and it reads as if you didn't listen. Write the rest yourself.

### What's the difference between a canned response and an auto-reply?

You choose to send a canned response, and you can edit it first. An auto-reply goes out on its own, like an away message when you're closed.
