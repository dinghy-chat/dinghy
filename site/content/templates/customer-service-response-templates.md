# Customer service response templates

These are for the replies that are hard to write. A complaint, an angry customer, an apology, a handoff to someone senior, a follow-up nobody asked for. Each situation has a short chat version and a longer email version. Copy one, fill in the brackets, and change at least one sentence so it's about this customer.

![Blank message cards laid out on a chart table with a compass, and a sailboat outside the porthole](/img/illustrations/response-templates.webp)

For quick everyday answers, like greetings or password resets, see the [canned responses](/templates/canned-responses).

## The placeholders

Fill in the words in curly brackets before you send:

- `{name}` is the customer's first name. Without it, start with "Hi" or "Hi there".
- `{your name}` is yours.
- `{issue}` names the problem in the customer's words, like "the late delivery" or "the export that dropped half your rows".
- `{what happened}` is the cause in a sentence or two, if you know it.
- `{what happened}` and `{fix}` are whole sentences, and the templates add the period. Write `{fix}` as what you've done or will do, like "I've sent a replacement" or "I'll reship it today".
- `{time}` is a real time, like "3pm" or "noon on Thursday", and `{date}` is a calendar date, like "Thursday, December 3".
- `{teammate}` is the other person on your team in a handoff.
- `{order number}` and `{amount}` are what they say.

Other brackets, like `{area}` or `{next step}`, name what goes in them.

## How to answer a complaint

The same four steps work for almost every complaint, in chat or by email.

1. Say what went wrong, in their words, so they know you read it.
2. Own your part. If it was your mistake, say so. If it wasn't, don't blame them either.
3. Say what you'll do, and when.
4. Do it, then tell them it's done.

Skip "We value your feedback" and "We apologize for any inconvenience". They read as a form letter, and the customer stops reading there.

## Complaints

Use these when a customer tells you something went wrong, and they're annoyed but not furious. Change `{what happened}` to the real cause if you know it. If you don't, say you're finding out. Don't guess.

### Chat

```template
Thanks for telling me, {name}. You're right, {issue} isn't how it should work. I'm fixing it now and I'll update you here by {time}.
```

```template
That's a fair complaint, so here's the straight answer. {what happened}. {fix}.
```

```template
I'm sorry about {issue}. I've checked, and it was our mistake. {fix}. Is there anything else it caused that I should sort out too?
```

### Email

```template
Subject: About {issue}

Hi {name},

Thanks for writing, and I'm sorry about {issue}. You shouldn't have had to deal with that.

{what happened}.

{fix}. If anything else went wrong because of this, reply to this email and I'll take care of it.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: Re: your complaint about order {order number}

Hi {name},

You're right to be annoyed. Order {order number} should have arrived on {date}, and it didn't.

{fix}. You'll get a tracking link by email as soon as it ships, and I'll check on it myself on {check date}.

Sorry again for the wait.

{your name}
```

## Angry customers

Use these when someone is upset, maybe shouting in capitals. Don't match their tone and don't defend yourself in the first line. Agree with what's true, then move to action. If they insult you or your team, you can say you'll keep helping and ask them to keep it civil. You don't have to take abuse.

### Chat

```template
I'm sorry, {name}. That's a frustrating thing to deal with, and I want to get it fixed. Can you tell me {question}?
```

```template
You're right, and I'm not going to make excuses. {fix}.
```

```template
I can see you've had to chase us about this, and that's on us. I've read everything so far, so you won't need to explain it again.
```

```template
I'm happy to keep working on {issue} with you until it's sorted. Please keep it civil, and we'll get there.
```

### Email

```template
Subject: Re: {issue}

Hi {name},

I read your message, and I get why you're angry. {what happened}. Then we took too long to answer. Both are on us.

{fix}.

You won't have to explain any of this again. I'll write by {time} with an update, even if we're still working on it.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: Re: {issue}

Hi {name},

I'm sorry. You've written to us three times about {issue}, and you shouldn't have had to.

I've looked at the whole thread. {what happened}. {fix}. I've tested it myself.

If it's still not right on your end, reply here and we'll take another look.

{your name}
```

## Apologies

Use these when you made a mistake. A good apology is short and specific. Name the mistake, say sorry once, fix it, and don't add "if you were offended" or "any inconvenience". If the mistake cost them money, say what you'll do about the money.

### Chat

```template
That was our mistake, and I'm sorry. {fix}.
```

```template
Sorry, I gave you the wrong answer earlier. The right one is {answer}. Thanks for checking.
```

```template
Sorry about the wait, {name}. We were short on people today. Here's the answer to your question:
```

### Email

```template
Subject: We got this wrong

Hi {name},

I'm sorry about what happened on {date}. {what happened}. That was our mistake.

{fix}. To make up for it, {what you're giving back}.

You don't need to do anything. If you see anything else that looks off, reply here.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: Sorry about the outage on {date}

Hi {name},

Our service was down for {length of outage} on {date}. If you tried to use it then, that's why it didn't work.

{what happened}. {what you've changed since}.

Sorry for the trouble.

{your name}
```

## Escalations

Use these when you need to pass a problem to someone more senior or more technical. The customer's worry is that they'll start from zero, so tell them they won't. Then leave your teammate a note with what's wrong, what you tried and what you promised.

### Chat

```template
I want to get this right, so I'm bringing in {teammate}, who looks after {area}. I've told them everything, so you won't have to repeat it. They'll reply here by {time}.
```

```template
This needs someone with access I don't have. I've passed it to {teammate} and marked it urgent. I'll stay on the chat until they pick it up.
```

```template
Hi {name}, I'm {your name}. {teammate} filled me in on {issue}. I'm looking at it now.
```

### Email

```template
Subject: Re: {issue}

Hi {name},

I've passed your message to {teammate}, who handles {area}. They can do things on your account that I can't.

They have the whole thread and my notes, so you won't need to explain it again. You'll hear from them by {time}.

Thanks for your patience with this.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: I'm taking over {issue}

Hi {name},

I'm {your name}, and I look after {area}. {teammate} told me about {issue} and I've read the thread.

{fix}. I'll write again by {time} with the result.

{your name}
```

## Follow-ups

Use these after you fixed something, or when you promised an update. A follow-up that arrives before the customer asks is the cheapest way to earn trust in support. Send it even when the news is "no news yet".

### Chat

```template
Hi {name}, just checking in about {issue}. Is everything sorted on your end?
```

```template
Quick update, I'm still working on {issue}. Nothing for you to do yet. Next update by {time}.
```

```template
Good news, I've taken care of {issue} on our side. Does that sort it for you?
```

### Email

```template
Subject: Update on {issue}

Hi {name},

A quick update, as promised. {status}.

Next, I'll {next step}. I'll write again by {time}.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: Is {issue} sorted?

Hi {name},

I wanted to check in about {issue}. Is everything sorted on your end?

If it is, no need to reply. If it isn't, answer this email and we'll pick it up again.

{your name}
```

```template
Subject: Fixed: {issue}

Hi {name},

The fix for {issue} is live. You don't need to do anything, but a refresh will make sure you have it.

Thanks for reporting it. Your steps made it much faster to find.

{your name}
```

## Chat or email

Chat replies should be two or three sentences. The customer is waiting with the window open, so get to the point and send. If the answer is long, send it in two or three short messages, not one wall of text.

Email has room for the whole story, so say what happened, what you did and what happens next. Put the most useful line first, because people read the first two lines in their inbox and decide from there.

In Dinghy, a chat and its emails are the same conversation. If your server sends mail and a visitor left their email, Dinghy emails them the replies they haven't read yet, so the chat version still reaches them. With [replies by email](/docs/self-hosting#replies-by-email) set up, they can answer from their inbox, right into the same conversation. Your team can store the versions you use most as [saved replies](/docs/operator-guide#saved-replies) and insert them by typing **/** in the reply box.

## Questions people ask

### How do you respond to an angry customer?

Agree with what's true, say sorry for the specific thing, and say what you'll do and by when. Don't defend yourself in the first line, and don't match their tone. Then do what you said and tell them it's done.

### How do you write a response to a customer complaint?

Repeat the problem in their words, own your part, say what you've done, and say what happens next. Keep it short. Leave out "We value your feedback" and "any inconvenience".

### Should you apologize if it isn't your fault?

You can be sorry they had a bad time without saying you caused it. "I'm sorry this has been such a hassle" is honest either way. Then explain what happened and help.

### How fast should you reply to a complaint?

In chat, within a few minutes, even if only to say you're looking into it. By email, the same working day. A quick "I'm on it" calms people more than a perfect reply the next day.

### When should you escalate a customer issue?

When you can't fix it yourself, when the customer asks for a manager, or when it involves money, data or legal risk. Tell the customer who takes over and when they'll hear back.
